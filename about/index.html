    About This Book¶

    Why This Book Exists¶

    Applying to college is one of the first big, high-stakes projects most students manage mostly on their own. It has dozens of deadlines, several online platforms and money decisions that can shape the next decade. Much of the process isn't written down anywhere, so students with a private counselor or a parent who has been through it start ahead.

    School counselors do essential work, but most have far too many students to walk each one through every step:

    • In the 2024–25 school year, the national average was 372 students for every school counselor, well above the 250-to-1 ratio the American School Counselor Association recommends.1
    • Even after students are admitted and commit, the process can fall apart over the summer. Researchers Benjamin Castleman and Lindsay Page found that 10 to 40 percent of college-intending students in the districts they studied didn't enroll anywhere that fall, a problem known as summer melt.2

    Behind those numbers are students who are capable of going to college but miss a deadline, misread an award letter or never learn a step exists. This book lays out the whole roadmap, so any student can run the process with confidence instead of panic.

    This is also an intelligent textbook, which makes it different from a printed guide. It's built on a learning graph of 342 concepts, mapped so that each idea comes after the ones it depends on. It includes 50 interactive MicroSims, browser-based tools where students try ideas with their own numbers, like calculating a GPA, checking a test score against a college's range or comparing award letters side by side. And it's free and open, with no paywall, no account and no access code.

    Who This Book Is For¶

    The book is written for high school students, mainly rising seniors and seniors, who are applying to four-year colleges in the United States. It assumes no prior knowledge of admissions or financial aid, so it works well for first-generation students. Parents, guardians and school counselors can read it alongside a student, because every chapter explains the reasons behind each step, not just the steps.

    How to Use This Book¶

    The chapters follow the order of senior year, so reading front to back gives the clearest picture. Each chapter also stands on its own, so you can jump to whatever is urgent. The book includes:

    • 20 chapters covering how admissions works, building and researching a college list, getting organized, testing, senior-year courses, activities, essays, recommendations, the Common App, interviews, financial aid, scholarships, award letters, waiting, decisions, the final choice and the summer before college
    • 50 interactive MicroSims embedded in the chapters
    • A 10-question quiz at the end of each chapter, 200 questions in all
    • Annotated references for each chapter, linking to Wikipedia, recommended books and trusted sources like studentaid.gov
    • A Glossary of 342 terms in plain language
    • An FAQ with 87 common questions and answers
    • Search from any page, using the search bar at the top

    The Learning Graph shows how the book's concepts connect. Start there if you want to explore on your own or check what a topic depends on.

    Admissions and financial aid policies change every year. Treat specific dates, dollar amounts and rules in this book as a starting point, and confirm them on each college's website and on studentaid.gov.

    About the Author¶

    Beth Kullberg is a product strategy and innovation leader who has spent more than two decades turning complicated systems into clear, usable roadmaps. She is passionate about helping people achieve their best life.

    From 2011 to 2026, Beth held senior leadership roles at UnitedHealth Group in Minnetonka, Minnesota. As Senior Director of UnitedHealthcare Consumer Product, she led the portfolio of digital tools and services members use to manage their health, including the member home page, login and registration, and caregiver strategy. At Optum, she directed the Advanced Technology Collaborative, managing a $24 million portfolio in data science, the Internet of Things and ambient computing. She also built the enterprise's partnership blueprint for co-innovation and helped lead its Lean Startup and innovation programs as an Entrepreneur-in-Residence. Before that, she spent eight years at MoneyGram International, directing global product platforms that served 200,000 agents in 190 countries.

    Beth holds a Bachelor of Science in Marketing, with a minor in Advertising, from St. Cloud State University. She also earned an executive certificate in data science from the University of Minnesota's Carlson School of Management, completed Carnegie Mellon University's executive program in artificial intelligence and machine learning, and is Pragmatic Marketing certified.

    Beth wrote College Admissions Roadmap to help her daughter manage her future goals. Along the way, she saw how much of the process depends on knowing steps that no one writes down, and how capable students can feel lost because of it. Applying to college is one of the first big decisions a young person makes about their own future, and Beth believes high school students deserve to lead it themselves, with the knowledge and confidence to make their own choices.

    The book brings the approach she has used throughout her career, mapping a confusing, high-stakes process into clear steps, to that moment. Her goal is to empower every student who reads it, whether or not they have a counselor or a parent who has been through it, to take this important step toward furthering their education on their own terms.

    How to Cite This Book¶

    If you reference this book in a lesson plan, curriculum proposal or other publication, please use one of these formats.

    APA (7th edition)

    Kullberg, B. (2026). College Admissions Roadmap. https://bakullberg.github.io/College-Admissions-ebook/

    Chicago (17th edition)

    Kullberg, Beth. 2026. College Admissions Roadmap. https://bakullberg.github.io/College-Admissions-ebook/.

    MLA (9th edition)

    Kullberg, Beth. College Admissions Roadmap. 2026, bakullberg.github.io/College-Admissions-ebook/.

    BibTeX

    @book{kullberg2026collegeadmissions,
  title     = {College Admissions Roadmap},
  author    = {Kullberg, Beth},
  year      = {2026},
  url       = {https://bakullberg.github.io/College-Admissions-ebook/},
  note      = {Interactive intelligent textbook}
}

    To cite a specific chapter, add the chapter number and title. For example:

    Kullberg, B. (2026). Chapter 1: The College Landscape and Application Rounds. In College Admissions Roadmap. https://bakullberg.github.io/College-Admissions-ebook/chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/
