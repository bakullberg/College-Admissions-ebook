    MicroSims¶

    MicroSims are small interactive simulations, each focused on one idea from the book. Every chapter embeds its sims, and each one also has its own lesson page with a fullscreen link and teaching notes.

    Every sim below is marked built (orange dot): implemented and awaiting the author's review.

    Chapter 1: The College Landscape and Application Rounds¶

    • College Admissions Process Roadmap: An interactive road map of the six stages of the college admissions process, with a drag-and-drop quiz on their order.
    • Senior Year Admissions Timeline: An interactive timeline of one senior year in admissions, with a personal track that changes as you choose Regular Decision, Early Action, or Early Decision.
    • College Type Sorter: A drag-and-drop sorting activity: classify twelve real colleges as public or private and as a research university or liberal arts college, then decide which public research universities are flagships.
    • Admission Rate Calculator: Calculate a college's admission rate from its applications and admits, and see why a surge in applications lowers the rate even when admits stay the same.
    • The Journey of an Application File: A swimlane flowchart that walks an application through an admissions office, showing who touches the file at each of seven steps.
    • Application Round Explorer: Answer five questions about one college and one student, and the explorer recommends an application round, explains why, and shows its deadline, decision, and commitment dates.

    Chapter 2: How Colleges Evaluate Applicants¶

    • Annotated Transcript Explorer: Explore a fictional high school transcript region by region and learn what each part tells an admissions reader.
    • GPA Calculator: Calculate an unweighted GPA from your own courses and see how credits, single grades, and a college's core-only recalculation change the result.
    • Rigor in Context: See why course rigor is judged against what your high school offers: compare the share of available advanced courses taken, not the raw count.
    • Holistic Review Rating Explorer: Compare applicant files with similar grades and see how a first reader's separate Academic, Activities, and Personal ratings respond to each part of the file, and to context.
    • Regent Admission Index Calculator: Run the University of Iowa's Regent Admission Index formula and see how GPA, ACT score, core courses, and residency decide whether you clear the admission threshold.
    • Middle 50% Range Plotter: Plot your GPA and ACT score against a college's middle 50% range and see whether you fall below, inside, or above it.

    Chapter 3: Finding Your Fit¶

    • College Self-Assessment Builder: A five-step self-assessment of majors, size, setting, culture, and cost that turns your answers into a ranked priority profile with dealbreakers.
    • Campus Size and Setting Explorer: Compare typical campus experiences across twelve combinations of school size and setting, highlight your target zone, and pin the colleges on your list.
    • Cost of Attendance Builder: Build a college's cost of attendance from tuition, room and board, and indirect costs, and compare how residency and housing choices change the total.
    • Reach, Target, Safety Classifier: Classify a college as a reach, target, or safety for you from your GPA and test score, its admission rate and middle 50% ranges, and its financial fit.
    • Balanced College List Builder: Build a college list of eight to twelve schools across reach, target, and safety, and check live whether it is balanced and includes an affordable safety you'd be glad to attend.

    Chapter 4: Researching and Narrowing Your College List¶

    • Research Tool Matrix: A matrix showing which college research tools best answer which questions, from admission chances and cost to campus culture.
    • College Outcomes Comparison: Compare retention, four-year, and six-year graduation rates for up to four colleges, and see which gaps are a reason to ask questions.
    • List Narrowing Funnel: Run Marcus's 18-college long list through three narrowing passes (fit, balance, and picturing yourself there) and watch each cut get logged with its reason.

    Chapter 5: Getting Organized¶

    • College Requirements Matrix: Compare application requirements across every college on your list, spot the one-of-a-kind requirements before they're missed, and total the workload.
    • Getting Organized Setup Checklist: An interactive checklist of the five first steps and the core setup tasks for getting organized, with a progress ring and a note on why each task matters.

    Chapter 6: The SAT and ACT Question¶

    • SAT-ACT Score Converter: Convert between SAT totals and ACT composites with the official concordance, and place the converted score in a college's middle 50% range.
    • Score Submission Planner: Compare your SAT or ACT score with every college on your list and get a college-by-college recommendation on whether to submit it, with the reason shown.
    • Superscore Calculator: Calculate an SAT or ACT superscore from several test dates and compare it with your best single-sitting score.

    Chapter 7: Your Senior Year Course Load¶

    • Advanced Course Pathways Compared: Compare Advanced Placement, the International Baccalaureate, and dual enrollment on sign-up, commitment, assessment, college credit, and fit, and find the pathway that matches your situation.
    • When Colleges See Senior Grades: A timeline of when colleges receive senior-year course and grade information, and how that timing differs for early, regular, and deferred applicants.
    • Senior Schedule Balance Planner: Build a draft senior schedule and weekly commitments, then compare the rigor signal it sends with the weekly hours it demands, to find a sustainable plan.

    Chapter 8: Activities and Resume¶

    • Depth vs. Breadth Activity Map: Plot activities by years of involvement and weekly hours to see the difference between a deep activity profile and a broad one, then plot your own.
    • Activity Entry Builder: Draft a Common App-style activity entry within the real character limits and get instant feedback on action verbs, specifics, and hours.
    • What Counts as an Activity?: Decide whether fourteen everyday commitments, from jobs and family duties to hobbies, belong on your activities list, and see which activity type each would go under.

    Chapter 9: The Personal Essay¶

    • Essay Topic and Prompt Matcher: Test a candidate essay topic against the qualities of a strong personal story, and see which Common App prompts it fits.
    • Show-Don't-Tell Practice: Practice telling a general claim apart from a specific showing sentence, then write and check your own showing revision.
    • Personal Essay Structure Explorer: See how the parts of a personal essay work together under three common structures, and plan your own essay with a 650-word budget.

    Chapter 10: Supplemental Essays¶

    • Why Us Specificity Checker: Test a Why Us essay draft for specificity: highlight generic phrases and specific details, and run the swap test with another college's name.
    • Supplement Planner: Enter every supplemental essay prompt on your list, group them by type to see where one core essay can be adapted, and schedule the writing backward from deadlines.

    Chapter 11: Letters of Recommendation¶

    • Recommender Comparison Tool: Compare possible teacher recommenders on how well they know you, how recently they taught you, subject, and reliability, and see which two would write the strongest, most complementary letters.

    Chapter 12: Filling Out the Common App and Coalition App¶

    • Pre-Submission Checklist: A college-by-college review of your Common App application before you press Submit, so common errors are caught first.

    Chapter 13: Interviews¶

    • Interview Practice Deck: Practice answering 24 common college interview questions out loud, with a timer, likely follow-ups, and a quick self-check.

    Chapter 14: Understanding Financial Aid¶

    • Financial Aid Map: An explorable map of where financial aid comes from (federal, state, institutional, private), the forms it takes, and which kinds are need-based or merit-based.
    • Need and Aid Calculator: See how cost of attendance, the Student Aid Index, and a college's aid package combine into demonstrated need, net price, and unmet need.

    Chapter 15: Scholarships¶

    • Scholarship Priority Calculator: Rank scholarships by expected dollars per hour of work, see how renewability and reusing an essay change the ranking, and fit the best ones into the hours you have.
    • Scholarship or Scam?: Practice spotting scholarship scams by deciding whether twelve realistic offers look legitimate or like a scam, and naming the sign that decides it.

    Chapter 16: Comparing Award Letters¶

    • Award Letter Decoder: Translate any financial aid award letter into standard numbers: sort each line into free, earned, or borrowed money, then see the real net price and out-of-pocket cost.
    • Award Letter Comparison Chart: Compare up to four financial aid offers side by side on first-year net price, out-of-pocket cost, projected four-year cost, and debt.

    Chapter 17: Waiting and Managing Anxiety¶

    • What Can I Control?: Sort common waiting-period worries into what you can act on and what's out of your hands, and turn each controllable worry into a small next step.

    Chapter 18: Handling Decisions¶

    • Decision Response Guide: An interactive guide to the next steps and deadlines for every kind of admission decision: acceptances, Early Decision outcomes, deferrals, waitlists, denials, and offers for a different program or term.

    Chapter 19: Making Your Final Choice¶

    • Final Choice Comparison Matrix: Compare two to four finalist colleges with a weighted decision matrix of cost, fit, outcomes, distance, and gut sense, and see which factors drive the result.

    Chapter 20: Getting Ready for Fall¶

    • Summer-to-Move-In Countdown: Organize every task between committing to a college and move-in day on a timeline, check them off, and watch for the summer-melt risks that keep admitted students from arriving.
    • Personal Application Roadmap: Build a complete, college-specific application roadmap with 19 stops, from list-building to fall move-in.

    Other tools¶
