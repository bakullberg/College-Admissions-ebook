    Quiz: Letters of Recommendation¶

    Test your understanding of choosing and asking recommenders, preparing materials for them, and the FERPA waiver with these review questions.

    1. At minimum, how far ahead of your first deadline does the chapter say to ask teachers for recommendations?¶

    1. Six to eight weeks
    2. Six months
    3. Three to four days
    4. One to two weeks
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter says to ask in the spring of junior year or at the very start of senior year, and at least six to eight weeks before your first deadline. For a November 1 deadline, that means mid-September at the latest. Teachers often receive many requests, and some limit how many they accept, so the earliest askers get the most time and attention. A week or a few days is far too little notice.

    2. According to the chapter, when should you send thank-you notes to your recommenders?¶

    1. Only if you're admitted to the colleges that received their letters
    2. Before they write the letter, to encourage a strong one
    3. Only after you've committed to a college on May 1
    4. After they submit their letters, and again when you hear your decisions
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter says to send short, sincere thank-you notes after recommenders submit their letters, and again when you hear your decisions. Writing a letter takes real time on top of a teacher's regular work. Following up in the spring to say where you were admitted and where you're going matters too, since teachers rarely hear how things turned out. The thanks isn't meant to influence the letter, and it doesn't depend on being admitted.

    3. Which teachers do many colleges prefer for teacher recommendations?¶

    1. Teachers from core academic subjects who taught you in 11th or 12th grade
    2. Teachers from any subject who taught you in 9th grade
    3. Teachers of elective courses, since they see creativity
    4. Teachers who gave you the highest grades in any year
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter says many colleges prefer teacher letters from core academic subjects, which are English, math, science, social studies, and world languages, from 11th or 12th grade. Recent teachers have seen your most mature work. Some programs, such as engineering or the arts, ask for one letter from a related subject. The chapter also stresses that how well a teacher knows you matters more than the grade you earned.

    See: Letters of Recommendation

    4. Why is the counselor recommendation especially important for explaining a student's situation?¶

    1. Counselors replace teacher letters at most selective colleges
    2. Counselors write letters only for students applying Early Decision
    3. Counselors are the only recommenders who can rate a student's essays
    4. Counselors see the whole high school record and can explain context like a move or family responsibility
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Unlike teachers, counselors see your whole high school record, including your course choices compared with what the school offers, your activities, and circumstances at home or school. That makes the counselor letter especially important for explaining context, such as a family responsibility, a move, an illness, a change in grades, or being first in your family to apply. Colleges that require letters almost always require it alongside teacher letters, not instead of them.

    See: Letters of Recommendation

    5. Why does the chapter suggest asking a teacher whether they would feel comfortable writing you a strong recommendation?¶

    1. It lets the teacher know they can see the student's application
    2. It reminds the teacher that colleges require strong letters
    3. It makes it harder for the teacher to say no to the request
    4. It gives a teacher who doesn't know you well an easy way to decline
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter says to give a teacher an easy way to decline, and this question does that. A teacher who doesn't know you well enough has room to say so gracefully. You want a letter from someone who says yes with enthusiasm, not one who feels obligated. The question isn't meant to pressure the teacher, and recommenders don't get access to your application through it.

    6. Why does the chapter say to make your FERPA waiver decision carefully before inviting any recommenders?¶

    1. The decision applies to every college and can't be changed once you've invited your first recommender
    2. Recommenders must sign the waiver on your behalf
    3. You can make a different choice for each college on your list
    4. The decision is private and colleges never learn which option you chose
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. On the Common App, your FERPA decision is shared with your recommenders and colleges, applies to every college on your list, and can't be changed once you've invited your first recommender. That's why the chapter says to decide before you invite anyone. It isn't private, it isn't set college by college, and the student, not the recommender, makes the choice.

    7. Devon is applying to engineering programs that need two teacher letters. Which pair best follows the chapter's advice on choosing recommenders?¶

    1. A 9th-grade art teacher and a family friend who is an engineer
    2. Whichever two teachers have the lightest workload this fall
    3. A junior-year physics teacher and a junior-year English teacher who both know Devon well
    4. Two math teachers from 9th and 10th grade who gave Devon A's
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's factors include how well the teacher knows you, recency, subject variety, relevance to your goals, and reliability. A junior-year physics teacher fits engineering, and a junior-year English teacher adds a different side of Devon, as long as both know him well. Older teachers chosen for high grades miss recency and variety. A family friend isn't a teacher recommendation, and teacher workload isn't one of the chapter's factors.

    8. A student's colleges have supporting document deadlines of November 1, November 15, and January 15. When should the student ask recommenders to submit letters through the Common App?¶

    1. Separately before each of the three deadlines
    2. By November 1, the earliest deadline, since one submission covers all assigned colleges
    3. By January 15, so recommenders have the most time
    4. After the student hears back from the earliest college
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter says to ask each recommender to submit by your earliest deadline whenever possible. Most letters submitted through the Common App go to all the colleges you've assigned at once, so one early submission covers everything. Waiting for the last deadline would make the letter late for the first two colleges. Track each letter in your tracker and send a polite check-in a week or two before each deadline if it hasn't been submitted.

    9. Which entry would be most useful on a brag sheet for a biology teacher?¶

    1. "I earned an A in your class and hope to major in biology."
    2. "In the March enzyme lab, our results didn't fit; I proposed we retest at three temperatures."
    3. "Please write that I am the best student you have ever taught."
    4. "I am a hardworking and curious student who loves science."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A brag sheet gives a recommender two or three specific moments from their class, what you learned, your goals, and anything that connects to your application. The enzyme-lab entry is a specific, accurate detail the teacher can use. General claims about being hardworking give the teacher nothing new, and the grade is already on your transcript. Telling a teacher what to write crosses the line, since the goal is to jog their memory, not write the letter for them.

    10. A teacher's letter says: "When our robotics code failed before regionals, Marcus sat with the two sophomores who wrote it and asked questions until they found the bug themselves." Why does a reader find this more valuable than "Marcus is diligent and a pleasure to have in class"?¶

    1. It focuses on the sophomores rather than on Marcus
    2. It mentions a competition, which shows the student won awards
    3. It shows a specific moment of leadership that only someone who knew Marcus could describe
    4. It is longer, and readers give more weight to longer letters
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter says the strongest letters share one trait: specificity. They describe particular moments that only someone who knows you could describe. The robotics passage shows leadership that builds other people up, which a reader will remember. The generic praise is positive, but nearly every letter says something similar. Length and awards aren't the point, and the passage is really about how Marcus acted.
