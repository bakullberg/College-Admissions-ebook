    3. 1. The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    Quiz: The College Landscape and Application Rounds¶

    Test your understanding of the admissions process, the kinds of four-year colleges, and the different application rounds with these review questions.

    1. For most students, about how long does the college admissions process run from start to finish?¶

    1. About three months, from the first application deadline to May 1
    2. About two years, from the start of junior year to the end of senior year
    3. About fifteen months, from the summer before senior year to the fall after graduation
    4. About six months, from the start of senior year to the last regular deadline
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter describes the process as running from the summer before senior year to the fall after graduation, about fifteen months. It includes all six stages: build your list, test, apply, seek aid, decide, and enroll. The shorter options cover only the application stage and leave out financial aid, the final decision, and enrollment steps like housing and orientation. That length is why many students feel behind, but it also makes the process forgiving.

    Concept Tested: College Admissions Process

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    2. Which application round is binding, meaning you must enroll if you are admitted?¶

    1. Restrictive Early Action
    2. Early Decision
    3. Early Action
    4. Rolling admission
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Early Decision is the only binding round in the chapter: if you are admitted, you must attend and withdraw your other applications, and you may apply to only one college this way. Early Action and rolling admission are non-binding. Restrictive Early Action limits where else you can apply early, but it is still non-binding, so an admitted student can decline the offer and decide by May 1.

    Concept Tested: Early Decision

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    3. What happens on May 1 in the admissions timeline?¶

    1. It is the most common deadline for Regular Decision applications
    2. It is the national deadline for choosing one college and paying a deposit
    3. It is the date when the Common App opens for the new year
    4. It is the date when most regular admission decisions are released
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. May 1 is the national deadline for choosing one college and paying a deposit. The other dates belong to different anchor points: the Common App opens August 1, most regular deadlines fall between January 1 and February 1, and most regular decisions arrive from mid-March to early April. Knowing these fixed points helps you see why the calendar is lopsided, with most work packed into August through January.

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    4. Iowa State University and the University of Iowa are both public research universities. Why is only the University of Iowa called the state's flagship?¶

    1. A flagship is any public university that offers both master's and doctoral degrees
    2. A flagship is the public university with the lowest in-state tuition
    3. A flagship is the best-known and usually oldest public research university in a state
    4. A flagship is the public university that enrolls the most students in the state
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A flagship university is the best-known and usually oldest public research university in a state, often carrying the state's own name. Every flagship is a public research university, but not every public research university is a flagship, which is why Iowa State does not qualify. Offering graduate degrees makes a school a research university, not a flagship. The chapter does not define flagships by tuition or enrollment size.

    Concept Tested: Flagship University

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    5. Why does the chapter recommend submitting an application a few days before the deadline?¶

    1. The deadline means the college must receive it, and servers often slow down on deadline night
    2. Colleges give extra points to applications that arrive before the deadline
    3. Early submission automatically moves your application into the early round
    4. Most colleges stop accepting applications several days before their posted deadline
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. An application deadline is the time by which the college must receive your application, not the time you start submitting it. The chapter notes that the Common App's servers are slow on deadline night nearly every year, and that is the student's problem, not the college's. Submitting a few days early is the only reliable protection. The chapter does not say colleges award extra points for early arrival or close before the posted date.

    Concept Tested: Application Deadline

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    6. A student is admitted to a private university through Restrictive Early Action. What was the trade-off of applying in that round?¶

    1. The student agreed to submit the application before the school's priority date
    2. The student agreed to skip comparing financial aid offers in the spring
    3. The student agreed not to apply early to most other private colleges
    4. The student agreed to enroll if admitted and withdraw all other applications
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Restrictive Early Action, also called Single-Choice Early Action, is non-binding, so an admitted student can still say no. In exchange for applying, the student agrees not to apply early to most other private colleges, though exact rules vary by school. Agreeing to enroll and withdraw other applications describes Early Decision, not REA. Because REA is non-binding, the student remains free to compare aid offers before May 1.

    Concept Tested: Restrictive Early Action

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    7. A college received 18,000 applications and admitted 2,700 students. What is its admission rate, and which selectivity band does it fall in?¶

    1. 15 percent, in the most selective band
    2. 6.7 percent, in the most selective band
    3. 25 percent, in the selective band
    4. 15 percent, in the highly selective band
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The admission rate equals students admitted divided by students who applied, times 100: 2,700 ÷ 18,000 × 100% = 15%. In the chapter's bands, 10 to 30 percent is highly selective, while the most selective band is under 10 percent. The 6.7 percent option divides the numbers the wrong way and misplaces the decimal. The 25 percent option is a miscalculation, and the selective band covers 30 to 60 percent anyway.

    Concept Tested: Admission Rate

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    8. A college has a 15 percent admission rate. Why doesn't that mean a particular applicant has a 15 percent chance of getting in?¶

    1. The rate only counts students who applied in the Regular Decision round
    2. The rate measures the quality of the college rather than how hard it is to get into
    3. The rate is reported only for out-of-state applicants at most universities
    4. The rate describes the whole applicant pool, and each student's odds depend on their own record
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter warns that an admission rate describes the whole applicant pool, not you. Your odds depend on how your academic record compares with the students the college actually admits. The rate measures selectivity, not quality, so that option reverses the chapter's point. The rate also covers all applicants, and the chapter notes it can differ by round and by program, which is another reason a single number cannot predict one student's chances.

    Concept Tested: Selective Admissions

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    9. What is rolling admission?¶

    1. A system in which a college reviews applications as they arrive and sends decisions until the class is full
    2. A system in which a college holds all applications until a single spring decision date
    3. A system in which a college moves deferred early applicants into the regular round
    4. A system in which a college admits every applicant who has a high school diploma
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Rolling admission means a college reviews applications as they arrive and sends decisions continuously, usually within a few weeks, until the class is full. It is non-binding and often has no single deadline, though many rolling schools set priority dates for scholarships or housing. Holding all applications for one date describes a round like Regular Decision. Admitting everyone with a diploma describes open admission, which is a selectivity level, not a review system.

    Concept Tested: Rolling Admission

    See: The College Landscape and Application Rounds

    10. A first reader notes that an applicant took all 3 AP courses her small rural high school offers and rates her academics highly. What does this note show about how admissions officers evaluate applications?¶

    1. Officers read a student's record against what that student's own high school offers
    2. Officers compare every applicant against the same fixed number of AP courses
    3. Officers care more about the number of courses taken than the grades earned
    4. Officers give extra credit to rural students regardless of their course choices
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Admissions officers are usually assigned a territory and know the high schools in it. They read your record against what your school offers, not against an ideal applicant. Three AP courses out of three offered is the maximum available, so the reader treated it as a demanding schedule. A fixed AP count would ignore that context. The note also cites her A/A- grades, so it does not suggest grades are ignored or that location alone earns credit.

    Concept Tested: Admissions Officer
