    Quiz: Getting Organized¶

    Test your understanding of trackers, filing systems, application accounts, and the people who help you with these review questions.

    1. How many colleges can a student add to a single Common App account?¶

    1. Up to 30
    2. Up to 20
    3. Up to 12
    4. Up to 10
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter says you can add up to 20 colleges on the Common App, which is far more than most students need. Eight to twelve is the usual size of a final college list, not the platform's limit. The Common App is accepted by more than 1,000 colleges, and each member college still sets its own deadlines, supplements, recommendation requirements, and testing policies, so you'll see a separate list of requirements for each one.

    Concept Tested: Common App

    See: Getting Organized

    2. What are the three principles that getting organized rests on, according to the chapter?¶

    1. One source of truth, visibility to the people helping you, and early buffers
    2. Paper backups, a printed calendar, and one deadline per college
    3. Daily checklists, color-coded folders, and weekly counselor meetings
    4. Applying early, submitting test scores, and using the Common App
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter names three principles. One source of truth means every deadline, document, and password has a single home. Visibility means a parent, guardian, or counselor can see the plan and catch a missed date. Early buffers means setting your own due dates a few days before the real ones, since portals crash and recommenders run late. The other options list tools or choices that aren't the chapter's organizing principles.

    Concept Tested: Getting Organized

    See: Getting Organized

    3. Which source does the chapter say you should treat as the final word on a college's specific requirements?¶

    1. The college's own admissions website
    2. The college's page on the Common App
    3. A third-party college search website
    4. A friend who applied to the college last year
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. College-specific requirements, such as extra essays, recommendations, testing rules, and portfolios, are found on the college's own admissions website, usually on a "first-year requirements" or "how to apply" page. The Common App lists each member's requirements, but the chapter says to treat the college's site as the final word because it's updated most directly. Policies change yearly, so a friend's experience last year can be out of date, as Emily's graded-paper example shows.

    Concept Tested: College-Specific Requirements

    See: Getting Organized

    4. Why does the chapter recommend using the same dedicated email address for every application and account?¶

    1. Using one address lets your counselor send documents automatically
    2. The Common App will only accept one email address per student
    3. Colleges give priority to applicants with professional email addresses
    4. Colleges match records partly by email, so one address keeps your file together
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter says to use the same address everywhere because colleges match records partly by email. A dedicated address also keeps college messages from getting buried, and you keep access after graduation, unlike a high school account. The chapter says a professional address looks better to admissions officers, but it doesn't say colleges give applicants priority because of it. Counselors send documents through the platform, not through your email.

    Concept Tested: Dedicated Email Address

    See: Getting Organized

    5. Why should checking applicant portals be a regular part of your routine?¶

    1. Colleges count each portal login as demonstrated interest
    2. Portals are the only place colleges post their application deadlines
    3. Missing or unmatched documents appear in the portal before anyone emails you, if anyone does
    4. Portals close permanently once the application deadline passes
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter says portals are where problems surface. A transcript that arrived but wasn't matched to your file, or a recommendation listed as missing, appears in the portal long before anyone emails you about it, if anyone does. That's why the chapter suggests checking about a week after each submission and again after each supporting document deadline. Deadlines belong in your tracker and come from the college's website, not only the portal.

    Concept Tested: Applicant Portal

    See: Getting Organized

    6. Which statement about independent counselors matches the chapter?¶

    1. They are reliable if they promise admission to specific colleges
    2. They are optional, and they never replace the school counselor, who still sends your official documents
    3. They are required for students applying to highly selective colleges
    4. They can submit your official transcript in place of your school
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. An independent counselor is a private advisor a family hires and pays. The chapter says plainly that you don't need one to apply successfully, and that one never replaces your school counselor, who still sends your official transcript and school profile. The chapter warns families to be wary of anyone who promises admission to specific colleges. It also points out that many community organizations offer free counseling, especially for first-generation and low-income students.

    Concept Tested: Independent Counselors

    See: Getting Organized

    7. Alex's legal name is Alexander, but everyone calls him Alex. How should he enter his name when he creates his Common App account?¶

    1. Enter whichever name is shorter, since colleges match files by email instead
    2. Enter his legal name exactly as on school records, and list "Alex" as a preferred name
    3. Enter "Alex" everywhere, since that's the name teachers know him by
    4. Enter "Alex" on the Common App and "Alexander" only on financial aid forms
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter says to use your legal name exactly as it appears on school records, test registrations, and financial aid forms, because mismatched names are a common cause of documents not being matched to your file. You can list a preferred name separately. Using different names on different forms creates exactly the mismatch the chapter warns about. Colleges match records partly by email, but not only by email, so the name still matters.

    Concept Tested: Common App Account

    See: Getting Organized

    8. A student's application is due November 15, and their personal due date is November 10. Following the chapter's two-reminder pattern, when should they set calendar reminders?¶

    1. November 3 and November 9
    2. November 1 and November 15
    3. November 8 and November 14
    4. November 10 and November 15
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter suggests two reminders for each deadline: one a week before your personal due date, to start finishing, and one the day before, to submit. With a personal due date of November 10, that means November 3 and November 9. The other options build reminders around the real deadline instead of the personal due date, which removes the early buffer the chapter recommends.

    Concept Tested: Calendar Reminders

    See: Getting Organized

    9. A college accepts both its own institutional application and the Common App. A student is already sending the Common App and a finished personal essay to six other colleges. Which choice does the chapter's reasoning support?¶

    1. Submit both applications to show extra interest in the college
    2. Use the institutional application, since it always requires an essay
    3. Use the Common App, since adding one more college takes little extra work
    4. Use the institutional application, since colleges prefer their own forms
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's Iowa example shows the trade-off. If a college is one of several on your list and you already have a Common App essay, the Common App is little extra work. If it were your only application, the shorter institutional application might be simpler. The chapter doesn't say colleges prefer their own forms or that you should submit both. Iowa's own application actually requires no personal essay for general admission.

    Concept Tested: Institutional Application

    See: Getting Organized

    10. Which family arrangement best matches the chapter's description of the parent and family role?¶

    1. A parent owns the tracker and submits applications to keep things on schedule
    2. A parent chooses the final college once offers arrive
    3. A parent rewrites the student's essays so they sound more polished
    4. A parent gathers tax documents and reads essay drafts only when asked
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter describes families as supporting, informing, and helping with specific tasks while the student leads. Parents usually provide financial information and complete parent sections of aid forms, and they read drafts if asked. Essays written by parents tend to sound like parents, and readers can tell, so rewriting takes the process away from the student. The student owns the tracker and makes the final choice, while family helps compare costs and offers.

    Concept Tested: Parent and Family Role
