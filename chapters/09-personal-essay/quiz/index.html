    Quiz: The Personal Essay¶

    Test your understanding of choosing a topic, writing techniques, and the drafting and revision process for the personal essay with these review questions.

    1. What are the word limits for the Common App personal essay?¶

    1. A maximum of 250 words, with a minimum of 150
    2. A maximum of 650 words, with a minimum of 250
    3. A maximum of 1,000 words, with a minimum of 500
    4. A maximum of 500 words, with no minimum
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The Common App personal essay has a maximum of 650 words and a minimum of 250, and the application box enforces the maximum. The chapter says 650 words is about a page and a third double-spaced, which leaves room for one story told well, not three told briefly. Most strong essays use roughly 550 to 650 words. It's fine to draft long and cut later, since many first drafts run 900 words or more.

    2. How many Common App essay prompts are there, and what does the last one allow?¶

    1. Ten prompts, and the last asks about a topic that makes you lose track of time
    2. Seven prompts, and the last asks about a background or identity
    3. Five prompts, and the last asks about an obstacle or failure
    4. Seven prompts, and the last allows any topic of your choice
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The Common App offers seven essay prompts, and prompt 7 allows any topic of your choice. The background or identity prompt is prompt 1, the obstacle prompt is prompt 2, and the lose-track-of-time prompt is prompt 6. The chapter stresses that the prompts are broad on purpose, so most good essays fit more than one, and prompt 7 exists for stories that don't fit the others neatly.

    3. Which three essay structures does the chapter say work well for most personal essays?¶

    1. Five-paragraph, compare-and-contrast, and persuasive
    2. Question-and-answer, list-based, and dialogue
    3. Narrative arc, montage, and idea-driven
    4. Thesis-driven, research-based, and chronological summary
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter names three structures. A narrative arc tells one story mostly in order with a turning point. A montage connects several short scenes through a common thread. An idea-driven essay explores a question or idea using examples from your life. The chapter says a personal essay doesn't need the five-paragraph format from English class, and a personal essay isn't an academic paper proving a thesis with sources.

    See: The Personal Essay

    4. What is the difference between revision and editing?¶

    1. Revision happens after submission, while editing happens before
    2. Revision reworks content, structure, and meaning, while editing makes sentences correct
    3. Revision only shortens the essay, while editing adds new scenes
    4. Revision is done by others, while editing is done by the writer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Revision is rethinking and reworking a draft by changing its content, structure, focus, or meaning, not just fixing sentences. Editing makes sentences correct, looking at clarity, wordiness, and word choice. The chapter puts it simply: editing makes sentences correct, and revision makes the essay better. Both are the writer's job, and revision often includes cutting, but it can also add detail or new reflection, as Ana's five drafts show.

    5. Why does the chapter say a small moment essay often works well?¶

    1. Small moment essays fit only prompt 7, which readers favor
    2. 650 words is enough to render one moment vividly and reflect on it, and it can stand out
    3. Short experiences are less likely to include personal details
    4. Readers prefer essays that avoid any reflection
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A small moment essay zooms in on one brief, specific experience, then reflects on it to reveal something larger. The chapter says it works because 650 words is exactly enough to render one moment vividly and reflect on it, and because a small, true moment often stands out among essays about big events. Reflection is still essential, and small moments are full of personal detail. They also aren't limited to one prompt.

    6. Which set of qualities does the chapter say makes a good personal story?¶

    1. Yours, specific, and revealing something about you
    2. Dramatic, unusual, and focused on a major achievement
    3. Recent, positive, and connected to your intended major
    4. Short, funny, and focused on a family member
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A good personal story is yours, meaning only you could tell it this way. It's specific, grounded in particular moments rather than a summary. And it reveals a quality, value, or change in you that the reader wouldn't otherwise know. The chapter points out that drama, achievement, and uniqueness of experience are not on the list. Many students have cared for a sibling or worked at a restaurant, and what makes a story distinctive is how you see it.

    7. Which opening line works best as an opening hook, according to the chapter's advice?¶

    1. "The night our power went out, I finished my brother's science project by flashlight and learned what a circuit really was."
    2. "Webster's Dictionary defines perseverance as continued effort despite difficulty."
    3. "Since the dawn of time, humans have wondered how the world works."
    4. "In this essay, I will describe how I became interested in science."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The strongest hooks start in a scene, raise a question, or offer a striking detail. The first option drops the reader into a specific moment and makes them curious. The chapter lists dictionary definitions, sweeping statements like "since the dawn of time," and summaries of what the essay will say as the openings readers see constantly. A good hook doesn't have to be clever. It just has to be specific.

    See: The Personal Essay

    8. A student wants feedback on a personal essay draft. Which approach follows the chapter's advice?¶

    1. Ask one parent to rewrite the weaker paragraphs in a more polished style
    2. Ask each reader only "Is it good?" so their answers are easy to compare
    3. Ask two or three chosen readers specific questions such as "Where did you lose interest?"
    4. Send it to eight readers and make every change they suggest
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter says two or three readers are usually enough, since more brings conflicting advice. It recommends specific questions, such as what the essay says about you and where the reader lost interest, instead of "Is it good?" Above all, stay the author and treat feedback as information, not instructions. Having someone rewrite your essay costs your authentic voice and is dishonest, since the Common App asks you to certify the work is your own.

    Concept Tested: Feedback on Essays

    See: The Personal Essay

    9. A student has finished proofreading an essay in a word processor. What final step does the chapter recommend before submitting?¶

    1. Rewrite the opening one more time to make it more dramatic
    2. Submit it immediately, since the word processor already checked spelling
    3. Run it through a thesaurus to replace common words with stronger ones
    4. Paste it into the Common App box and read it there, since formatting can change
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter says to paste the essay into the Common App box and read it there, because formatting such as italics, paragraph breaks, and special characters sometimes changes when you paste. You should also check the word count in the box itself and save the exact submitted version as "FINAL — submitted." Replacing words with thesaurus choices would hurt your authentic voice. Proofreading is the last pass for typos, not a time for new rewrites.

    10. Two students write about the same championship basketball game. One describes the winning shot. The other describes noticing, from the bench, that a teammate was hiding an injury. What does the chapter's test for overused topics suggest?¶

    1. Both essays are equally strong, since they share the same prompt
    2. Both essays should be dropped, since sports topics are forbidden
    3. The winning-shot essay is stronger, since it describes the bigger moment
    4. The bench essay is stronger, since only that student could have written it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The big game is an overused topic because readers have seen thousands of winning shots. The chapter says overused topics aren't forbidden, but they need a fresh angle, such as an unexpected moment you noticed on the bench. Its test is that if a hundred students could write your essay with only the names changed, you need a more specific angle. The bench essay passes that test, while the winning-shot essay probably doesn't.
