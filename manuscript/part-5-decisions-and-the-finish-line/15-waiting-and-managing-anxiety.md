# 15. Waiting & Managing Anxiety

The stretch between submitting your last application and hearing back is, for most students, the hardest part of the whole process — not because there's more work to do, but because there's suddenly none, and nothing left to control.

## What Actually Happens to an Application After Submission

Once submitted, your application typically moves through a sequence roughly like this:

1. **Processing** — the school confirms receipt of every required piece (application, transcript, recommendations, test scores, fee) and checks your file is complete. This alone can take a few weeks, especially in the busiest weeks around January deadlines.
2. **First read** — an admissions officer, often one assigned to your specific high school or region, reads your full file and typically writes a summary and preliminary rating.
3. **Committee review** — many schools bring files to a committee, especially borderline or particularly compelling cases, where readers can advocate for an applicant.
4. **Shaping the class** — especially late in the process, decisions aren't made purely file-by-file; the school is also balancing the incoming class as a whole across major, geography, and institutional priorities. This is part of why strong applicants sometimes don't get in, and it has nothing to do with anything you could have controlled.
5. **Decisions released** — on the school's published timeline (immediately for rolling admission, at a fixed date for regular decision).

Nothing about your file changes after submission (short of a wildly different final transcript) — the waiting period genuinely is just waiting, not a phase where more effort would help.

## How to Stay Sane During the Wait

- **Stay busy with things unrelated to admissions.** This is not avoidance — it's accurate, since there is nothing left to do about your applications, and filling the time with checking portals only manufactures more anxiety.
- **Limit portal-checking to a set time**, once a day at most. Most decisions don't trickle in gradually; they arrive on specific release dates, so constant checking mostly just produces stress with no new information.
- **Watch your social media intake.** Seeing peers post acceptances (and rarely, by nature, their rejections) can distort your sense of how things are actually going. It's fine to mute or take a break from admissions-related accounts during this window.
- **Talk about the anxiety, don't just sit with it.** Parents, friends going through the same wait, or a counselor can all help — this is a genuinely stressful, liminal period for almost everyone going through it, not a sign that you're handling it unusually badly.
- **Prepare yourself for all outcomes in advance**, not just the best one. Revisit your list (chapter 2) and remind yourself why your safety and target schools made the cut — it softens the blow of any single rejection to already know you have real, wanted options regardless of how any one decision goes.

The waiting period ends. What matters is getting through it without the anxiety itself becoming the whole experience of senior year.
