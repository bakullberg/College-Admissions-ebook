# 17. Making Your Final Choice

Once every decision is in and every aid offer has been compared and, where appropriate, appealed, it's time to pick one school. This chapter is about making that choice deliberately, not by default or by deadline pressure.

## Comparing Offers Side by Side

Build one simple comparison table — a continuation of the tracker from chapter 3 — with a row for each school still in contention and columns for:

- **Net price** (from chapter 14's calculation — the real number, not the sticker price)
- **Academic fit** — does your intended major (or the flexibility to explore, if undecided) look strong here?
- **Social and campus fit** — from your original self-assessment in chapter 2, does what you learn from visiting confirm or challenge your earlier impression?
- **Outcomes** — post-graduation employment or graduate school rates in your field of interest, if available.
- **Gut read** — how did you feel when you visited or pictured yourself there?

Weigh these factors according to what actually matters to you, not according to some external ranking. A school ranked higher nationally isn't automatically the better choice for your specific goals, budget, and preferences.

## Revisiting Campuses

If you're deciding between two or three finalists and haven't visited recently (or at all), a revisit — often through an admitted-students day — is worth the time and expense if at all feasible. Seeing a campus as an admitted student, sitting in on a class, and talking to current students in your intended major gives you information no brochure or virtual tour can replicate. Pay attention to your reaction beyond the organized program: eat a meal in the dining hall, walk around outside the tour route, and notice how you feel simply existing in that space for a few unstructured hours.

If a revisit isn't feasible, look for a virtual admitted-students event, or ask the admissions office to connect you with a current student in your intended major for an honest conversation.

## Committing by May 1

**May 1** is the near-universal deadline (sometimes called "National College Decision Day") by which admitted students must submit an enrollment deposit to hold their spot. Missing it can mean losing your spot entirely, so treat it as a hard deadline, not a soft one.

Before May 1:

- Confirm your final choice using the comparison above.
- Submit your enrollment deposit and any required intent-to-enroll form.
- Formally decline every other offer — a simple, polite notification through each school's portal — as a courtesy that also frees up your (now unneeded) waitlist or admitted spot for another student.
- If you were counting on a waitlist decision that hasn't come through yet, deposit at your best confirmed option regardless (chapter 16) — you can typically still switch later if the waitlist comes through, but you're never left without a plan in the meantime.

Once you've deposited, the choice is made. Give yourself permission to feel settled in it — a small amount of lingering "what if" is normal, but by this point you've made a deliberate, well-informed decision, and it's time to shift your energy toward chapter 18: getting ready to actually go.
