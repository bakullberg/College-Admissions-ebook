# 3. Getting Organized

The college process doesn't fail because of any single hard step — it fails because of dropped threads. A missed deadline, a recommender who never got your resume, a login you can't remember in December. This chapter is about building the scaffolding that keeps that from happening.

## Deadline Trackers

Build one tracker — a spreadsheet or shared doc — the same week you finalize your list, and keep it as the single source of truth. At minimum, track for each school:

- Application deadline (and whether it's ED, EA, or RD)
- Required supplemental essays and their word counts
- Number and type of recommendations required
- Testing policy and whether you're submitting scores
- Financial aid deadlines (FAFSA, CSS Profile, and any school-specific forms)
- Interview availability, if offered
- Portal login and password

Share this tracker with a parent or guardian and your counselor. A second set of eyes catches the deadline you missed because you were staring at it too closely to notice.

Set your own internal deadlines a few days ahead of the real ones. Every application has a way of taking longer than expected in the final 48 hours — portals get overloaded, recommenders submit late, files won't upload. A buffer turns a potential crisis into a non-event.

## Accounts to Set Up

- **Common App** — accepted by nearly 1,000 colleges; one core application, plus school-specific supplements.
- **Coalition App** (now part of Scoir at many schools) — an alternative to the Common App, used by a smaller but overlapping set of schools.
- **Individual school portals** — some schools (many public universities, in particular) use their own application system instead of, or in addition to, the Common App.
- **FAFSA account (FSA ID)** — set this up early; see chapter 12.
- **CSS Profile account**, if any of your schools require it.
- **Scholarship platforms**, as you identify them in chapter 13.

Use a password manager, or at minimum a single secure document, to store every login. You will create more accounts in this process than you expect, and you will need to get back into several of them months after you first set them up.

## A Simple Filing System

Create one folder (digital, physical, or both) with these subfolders:

- **Transcripts** — official and unofficial copies as you request them.
- **Test scores** — score reports from the College Board or ACT.
- **Essays** — drafts of your personal statement and every supplemental essay, organized by school.
- **Recommendations** — the resume and brag sheet you give to recommenders, and confirmation once each letter is submitted.
- **Financial documents** — tax returns and other paperwork you'll need for the FAFSA and CSS Profile.
- **Login info** — or a pointer to your password manager.

The investment here is small — an afternoon, maybe two — but it pays off every single week for the rest of the process. Getting organized isn't busywork before the "real" work starts; it's what keeps the real work from turning into a scramble.
