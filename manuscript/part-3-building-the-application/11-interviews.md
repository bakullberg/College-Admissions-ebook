# 11. Interviews

Not every school offers an interview, and where they do, it's rarely the deciding factor. But a good interview can add real color to your application — and a poorly prepared one is an easy, avoidable miss.

## When They Matter

Interviews generally fall into a few categories:

- **Evaluative interviews**, usually with an admissions officer or trained alumni interviewer, that factor directly into your application review.
- **Informational interviews**, usually with a current student or alum, meant more for you to learn about the school than for them to evaluate you — though a poor showing can still leave a negative impression that makes its way back.
- **Optional interviews**, offered but not required, which are almost always worth doing if available — they're a low-risk way to add a data point in your favor, and skipping an optional interview at a school that offers one is sometimes read as a mild signal of lower interest.

Check each school's specific policy — some don't offer interviews at all, in which case skip this chapter for that school and don't worry about it.

## How to Prepare

- **Know your own application.** Be ready to talk naturally about your activities, your intended major, and why you're interested in that specific school — the same material as your supplemental essays, but spoken rather than written.
- **Research the school specifically**, the same way you would for a "why us" essay — a program, a class, a specific opportunity you'd want to pursue there.
- **Prepare a few genuine questions to ask.** Interviewers almost always leave time for this, and "no questions" reads as a lack of real interest. Ask something you couldn't have answered from the school's website — a question about the interviewer's own experience there is usually a safe, effective choice.
- **Do one practice run**, out loud, with a parent, teacher, or friend — not to memorize answers, but to get comfortable hearing your own voice talk through your story before it matters.
- **Handle logistics ahead of time.** If it's a video call, test your camera, microphone, and internet connection the day before, not the minute it starts.

## What Colleges Are Actually Listening For

Interviewers are rarely trying to catch you out or test factual knowledge. They're listening for:

- **Genuine curiosity and engagement** — do you talk about your interests with real energy, or recite rehearsed lines?
- **Self-awareness** — can you reflect honestly on a challenge or a mistake, not just list accomplishments?
- **Fit** — does the way you talk about yourself suggest you'd contribute something specific to their community?
- **Basic conversational ease** — not polish, just the ability to have a normal, two-way conversation rather than a monologue or a series of one-word answers.

You don't need a perfect, scripted performance. Interviewers generally write notes summarizing their overall impression, not a transcript — a genuine, engaged conversation, even with some nerves and imperfect sentences, reads far better than something stiff and over-rehearsed.
