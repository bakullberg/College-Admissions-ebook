# 10. Filling Out the Common App / Coalition App

The Common App (and Coalition App) is straightforward for most of its sections, but a few spots trip up nearly every applicant. This chapter is a walkthrough of those specific sections — not the whole form, just the parts worth slowing down for.

## Profile and Family Sections

Fill these out early and carefully — this is where a surprising number of typos slip in (a misspelled address, a wrong graduation year) that then get copied into every school-specific submission. Double-check:

- Legal name matches your official documents (transcripts, test scores) exactly — mismatches here can cause processing delays.
- Your school's CEEB code (a 6-digit code identifying your high school) is correct; your counselor can confirm it.
- Family information is complete, including any colleges your parents or siblings attended — some schools use this for legacy consideration.

## Education Section

List every high school you've attended, even briefly, and every college course you've taken (dual enrollment, summer programs). Leaving off a summer course you took at a local college — even if it seems minor — can look like an omission if the college later finds it on an official transcript.

## Testing Section

Only report scores here for schools where you've decided to submit (see chapter 4) — and remember that self-reported scores here are separate from official score reports, which are usually sent directly from the College Board or ACT to each school. Check each school's specific policy on whether self-reported scores are sufficient at the application stage or whether official reports are required.

## Activities Section

This is the section covered in depth in chapter 6 — the trip point here is simply running out of room. The description field is short (150 characters); write it, cut it, and cut it again until it's as tight and specific as it can be. Lead with your best, most developed activities first — the order you list them in is itself a signal of what you consider most important.

## Writing Section

The personal essay goes here (chapter 7), along with a section for "additional information" — an optional field, not a second essay. Use it only if there's something the rest of the application doesn't explain: a gap in your transcript, an extenuating personal or family circumstance, or a brief clarification. Don't use it to restate your resume or pad your file with more of the same; an empty additional-information field is completely normal and not a red flag.

## Courses & Grades (Self-Reported)

Some schools require a self-reported transcript in addition to (or sometimes instead of) an official one. This is tedious — you're manually entering every course and grade from ninth grade on — but accuracy matters: discrepancies between your self-report and your official transcript, once it arrives, can raise questions. Do this section with your actual transcript open next to you, not from memory.

## School-Specific Questions and Supplements

Once your core application is complete, each school's supplement (chapter 8) appears as a separate section per school. Work through supplements school by school rather than trying to answer the same question type across all schools at once — it's easier to stay in the right mental context for "why this school" when you're focused on one school at a time.

## Before You Submit

For every school, before hitting submit:

- Preview the exact PDF the college will see — formatting sometimes shifts unexpectedly (line breaks, character limits) between the entry form and the final document.
- Confirm every required section shows as complete, including fee waiver or payment.
- Confirm your recommenders and counselor have been correctly assigned to that specific school in the system.

Submission confirmations can take a few minutes to a few hours to appear — don't panic immediately if it isn't instant, but do check back to confirm it went through before you consider that application done.
