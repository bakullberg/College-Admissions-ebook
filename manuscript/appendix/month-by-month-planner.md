# Appendix: Month-by-Month Senior Year Planner

This planner turns the whole book into a checklist tied to the real senior-year calendar. Each month lists what to actually be doing, with a pointer back to the chapter that covers it in depth. Treat exact dates as approximate — always confirm the specific deadlines for your own schools in your tracker (chapter 3).

## August–September

- Finalize your college list of 8–12 schools (chapter 2).
- Start your Common App and/or Coalition App account, and begin the profile sections (chapter 10).
- Request transcripts from your counseling office.
- Begin brainstorming your personal essay (chapter 7) — start collecting material even if you're not ready to draft.
- Confirm your senior-year course load is set and appropriately rigorous (chapter 5).

## October

- Take or retake the SAT/ACT if your testing plan calls for it (chapter 4).
- Draft your personal essay and begin supplemental essays for your earliest-deadline schools (chapters 7–8).
- Request letters of recommendation if you haven't already (ideally this happened even earlier, per chapter 9).
- Submit Early Action/Early Decision applications as their deadlines arrive (chapter 1 explains the difference; chapter 10 walks through the form itself).

## November

- Finish supplemental essays for remaining schools (chapter 8).
- Submit regular-decision applications with early (rolling or priority) deadlines.
- The FAFSA opens for the upcoming academic year — file it as soon as possible (chapter 12).

## December

- Continue completing and submitting regular-decision applications.
- Confirm your recommenders have submitted their letters for every school (chapter 9).
- Watch for mid-year grade report requests from schools — most require an updated transcript once first-semester grades post.
- If you applied ED, you'll typically hear back this month; see chapter 16 for how to handle the decision.

## January

- Submit any remaining regular-decision applications — this is the most common deadline month.
- Meet CSS Profile deadlines for schools that require it (chapter 12).
- Apply for outside scholarships as you identify them (chapter 13).

## February

- Finish remaining scholarship applications.
- Respond promptly to any financial aid office follow-up requests (verification documents, clarifications) — delays here can delay your award letter.
- This is a natural lull in application work; a good month to rest before decisions start arriving (chapter 15).

## March

- Decisions start arriving from many regular-decision schools.
- Begin comparing offers — both admission and financial aid — as they come in (chapters 14 and 16).
- Continue practicing the waiting strategies from chapter 15 for schools you haven't heard from yet.

## April

- Compare final financial aid packages across all your offers (chapter 14).
- Visit or revisit your top remaining schools if at all possible (chapter 17).
- Decide and prepare to commit before May 1.

## May

- Send your enrollment deposit to your chosen school (chapter 17).
- Formally decline every other offer.
- Take any AP exams you're registered for.
- Send your final transcript request to your chosen school once the school year ends (chapter 18).

## June–August

- Complete housing forms as early as your school allows (chapter 18).
- Register for and complete orientation, including initial course registration.
- Complete health requirements (immunizations, physical exam) ahead of any deadlines.
- Connect with your roommate, if assigned, before move-in.
- Confirm your final transcript was received and any AP credit has been properly applied.
- Prepare for move-in day — and for the transition itself, which is as much emotional as logistical (chapter 18).
