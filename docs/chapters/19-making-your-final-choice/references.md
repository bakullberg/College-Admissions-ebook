# References: Making Your Final Choice

1. [Decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-making) - Wikipedia - Overview of how people make choices, including common biases, background for the chapter's structured approach to choosing a college. It also explains common decision traps.

2. [Decisional balance sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decisional_balance_sheet) - Wikipedia - Explains the method of listing and weighing pros and cons of each option, the idea behind the chapter's Final Choice Matrix MicroSim.

3. [Student debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_debt) - Wikipedia - Covers student debt levels and their long-term effects, context for weighing total debt at graduation in a final decision. Include it in every side-by-side comparison.

4. Decisive: How to Make Better Choices in Life and Work - Chip Heath and Dan Heath - Crown Business (2013) - The Heaths' WRAP process (widen your options, reality-test assumptions, attain distance and prepare to be wrong) gives students a clear method for comparing finalists and testing them on campus revisits.

5. The Paradox of Choice: Why More Is Less - Barry Schwartz - Ecco (2004) - Schwartz's distinction between "maximizers," who chase the perfect option, and "satisficers," who choose a good one, helps students make a final choice without endless second-guessing.

6. [How to Decide Which College to Attend](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/plan-for-college/find-your-fit/how-to-decide-which-college-to-attend) - College Board BigFuture - Guidance on comparing admitted colleges on cost, fit and outcomes, matching the chapter's side-by-side comparison section. It includes questions to ask on admitted student days.

7. [Accepting Financial Aid](https://studentaid.gov/complete-aid-process/accept-aid) - Federal Student Aid - Official steps for accepting or declining parts of an aid offer, needed when committing to a college. Do this in each college's portal after you commit.

8. [College Scorecard](https://collegescorecard.ed.gov/) - U.S. Department of Education - Compare finalists on graduation rates, typical debt and earnings after graduation. Use the data to test whether a finalist's outcomes match its reputation.

9. [Your Financial Path to Graduation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/paying-for-college/your-financial-path-to-graduation/) - Consumer Financial Protection Bureau - Tool for turning an aid offer into a full cost and debt estimate, helpful for comparing offers before May 1.

10. [Is College Worth It?](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/pay-for-college/get-started/is-college-worth-it) - College Board BigFuture - Data on the long-term value of a degree, useful for weighing cost against outcomes in a final decision. Weigh it alongside the debt estimate for each finalist.
