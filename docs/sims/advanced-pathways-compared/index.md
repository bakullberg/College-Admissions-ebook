## About This MicroSim

Help students compare AP, IB, and dual enrollment on the dimensions that matter for admissions and credit, and match a pathway to their own situation.

**Learning objective:** Students will compare AP, IB, and dual enrollment and differentiate among them by structure, commitment, how they are assessed, and how college credit is earned.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 7: Your Senior Year Course Load](../../chapters/07-senior-year-course-load/index.md).

## How to Use

- Read across a row to compare the three pathways on one dimension. Click any cell for a fuller explanation.
- Click a column header for a one-paragraph summary of that pathway.
- Answer the three **Which fits me?** questions. The best-matching pathway is highlighted in gold with the reason.
- On narrow screens, use the **AP**, **IB**, and **DE** buttons to switch between pathways.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare AP, IB, and dual enrollment and differentiate among them by structure, commitment, how they are assessed, and how college credit is earned.

**Bloom level:** Analyze (L4) — compare, differentiate

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 7](../../chapters/07-senior-year-course-load/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Row by row (4 min):** Compare the College credit row. Why does every pathway end with "set by each college"?
2. **Sam's choice (3 min):** Using the Commitment and Watch out for rows, explain why Sam chose individual IB courses instead of the full Diploma.
3. **Your fit (3 min):** Answer the three questions for yourself and check whether the recommendation matches your plan.

### Discussion Questions

- Why might a dual-enrollment grade matter more later than an AP class grade?
- When is the IB Diploma worth its extra hours?
- How does your school profile help a college read whichever pathway you choose?

### Assessment

Students choose a pathway for a described student and justify it using two rows of the comparison.
