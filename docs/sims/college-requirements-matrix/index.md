## About This MicroSim

Show students how requirements differ across the colleges on one list, so they can spot unique requirements before they're missed and plan the total workload.

**Learning objective:** Students will compare application requirements across colleges and organize them to identify which requirements are shared and which are unique to one college.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 5: Getting Organized](../../chapters/05-getting-organized/index.md).

## How to Use

- Each row is a college, and each column is a requirement type. Gray = not required, blue = required and shared, gold = required by only one college.
- Hover over any cell or header for an explanation. Click a header to sort by that column.
- Click a row to select it, then **Edit selected** or **Remove selected**. **Add college** opens an editor for a new row (up to 10).
- Check **Highlight unique requirements only** to dim everything except the gold cells. **Load Emily's list** restores the chapter example.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare application requirements across colleges and organize them to identify which requirements are shared and which are unique to one college.

**Bloom level:** Analyze (L4) — compare, organize

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 5](../../chapters/05-getting-organized/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Find the gold (3 min):** With Emily's list loaded, find the unique requirement and explain why it would be easy to miss.
2. **Count the work (4 min):** How many supplemental essays does Emily's list require? Which three colleges account for most of them?
3. **Your list (8 min):** Replace Emily's colleges with your own, reading each college's own first-year requirements page.

### Discussion Questions

- Why might a requirement appear only on a college's own website and not on its Common App page?
- How can you write fewer, better supplemental essays across a long list?
- Why do teacher letters rarely multiply your work even when ten colleges ask for them?

### Assessment

Students build a matrix for their own list and name every unique requirement and its deadline.
