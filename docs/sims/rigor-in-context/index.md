## About This MicroSim

Show that course rigor is judged relative to what a high school offers, not as an absolute count of advanced courses.

**Learning objective:** Students will compare course schedules across high schools with different offerings and differentiate between the number of advanced courses a student took and the rigor that number represents.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 2: How Colleges Evaluate Applicants](../../chapters/02-how-colleges-evaluate/index.md).

## How to Use

- Set **Advanced courses offered** for the school and **Advanced courses taken** for the student.
- Gray tiles are the school's offerings; blue tiles are the courses the student took.
- Read the share bar and the five-band counselor rating. Hover over a band to see what it suggests to a reader.
- Press **Elena**, **Jordan**, or **Emily** to load the chapter's examples.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare course schedules across high schools with different offerings and differentiate between the number of advanced courses a student took and the rigor that number represents.

**Bloom level:** Analyze (L4) — compare, differentiate

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 2](../../chapters/02-how-colleges-evaluate/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Same count, different rigor (3 min):** Load Elena and Jordan. Both took three advanced courses. Explain why their ratings differ.
2. **Find the thresholds (4 min):** With 20 courses offered, find the smallest number taken that reaches each band.
3. **Your school (3 min):** Look up how many AP, IB, or dual-enrollment courses your school profile lists and enter your own schedule.

### Discussion Questions

- Is a student penalized for attending a school with few advanced courses? Why not?
- What does this model leave out that a real counselor would consider?
- Why does Jordan's counselor rate Jordan higher than the model does?

### Assessment

Students explain, using the share of available courses taken, why two students with the same number of AP courses receive different rigor ratings.
