## About This MicroSim

Show how an application moves through an admissions office and which people touch it at each step, so students understand who reads their file and why clarity matters.

**Learning objective:** Students will explain the path an application takes through an admissions office and summarize the role of the admissions officer at each step.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 1: The College Landscape and Application Rounds](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md).

## How to Use

- Click any step to read what happens there and who does it.
- Press **Follow a file**, then **Next ▶** to move the file icon one step at a time; each step's description opens automatically.
- Check **Formula-based public university** to see how step 4 shrinks to a brief check when a college admits by an index threshold.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will explain the path an application takes through an admissions office and summarize the role of the admissions officer at each step.

**Bloom level:** Understand (L2) — explain, summarize

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 1](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Follow one file (4 min):** Step through all seven steps and list the five kinds of people who handle the file.
2. **Compare two colleges (3 min):** Turn on Formula-based public university and explain which step changes and why.
3. **Write for the reader (3 min):** The first reader spends roughly ten to fifteen minutes on a file. Write one sentence about what that means for how you present your activities.

### Discussion Questions

- Why is a file not 'complete' until operations staff have matched every document?
- What does the first reader write, and who reads it next?
- How does the path differ at a formula-based public university?

### Assessment

Students describe the path of a file in order and name the role of the first reader at the first-read and committee steps.
