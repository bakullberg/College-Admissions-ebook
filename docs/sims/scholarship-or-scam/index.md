## About This MicroSim

Give students practice recognizing scholarship scam warning signs by classifying realistic offers as legitimate or suspicious.

**Learning objective:** Students will detect warning signs in scholarship offers and classify each offer as likely legitimate or likely a scam, naming the sign that decided it.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 15: Scholarships](../../chapters/15-scholarships/index.md).

## How to Use

- Read the offer, then press **Looks legitimate** or **Looks like a scam**.
- The feedback highlights the deciding phrase and names the warning signs or the signs of legitimacy.
- One card is genuinely unclear. It asks you to check the sponsor before trusting it.
- Press **Next ▶** for the next offer and **Restart** to shuffle. The panel collects every warning sign you've found. All sponsors are fictional.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will detect warning signs in scholarship offers and classify each offer as likely legitimate or likely a scam, naming the sign that decided it.

**Bloom level:** Analyze (L4) — detect, classify

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 15](../../chapters/15-scholarships/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Play the deck (6 min):** Classify all twelve offers.
2. **Name the signs (2 min):** From memory, list the seven warning signs from the chapter.
3. **Check a real offer (2 min):** If you've received a scholarship email, check it against the list and search the sponsor's name with "scam."

### Discussion Questions

- Why is any fee a warning sign, even a small one?
- Why should you never pay to file the FAFSA?
- What can you do to check an unfamiliar sponsor?

### Assessment

Students correctly classify at least ten of the eleven clear-cut offers and name the deciding warning sign for each scam.
