## About This MicroSim

Show students what a high school transcript contains and what an admissions reader takes from each part, so the document stops looking like an unreadable grid.

**Learning objective:** Students will identify the main regions of a high school transcript and explain what each one tells an admissions reader.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 2: How Colleges Evaluate Applicants](../../chapters/02-how-colleges-evaluate/index.md).

## How to Use

- Hover over the transcript to see each region outlined, with a one-line tooltip.
- Click a region to read the full explanation in the panel.
- Press **Read it like an officer**, then **Next ▶** and **◀ Back**, to step through the regions in the order a reader usually scans them.
- The GPA box is calculated from the course list on the page: A = 4, A- = 3.67, B = 3. The weighted GPA adds +1.0 for AP and +0.5 for Honors.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will identify the main regions of a high school transcript and explain what each one tells an admissions reader.

**Bloom level:** Understand (L2) — identify, explain

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 2](../../chapters/02-how-colleges-evaluate/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Tour (4 min):** Step through the officer tour and write one word for what each region tells the reader.
2. **Find the trend (3 min):** Compare Priya's grade 9, 10, and 11 tables. Is her record steady, rising, or falling as courses get harder?
3. **Check your own (3 min):** Request an unofficial copy of your transcript and find each of the nine regions on it.

### Discussion Questions

- Why do colleges recalculate GPA instead of using the number on the transcript?
- What does a reader learn from the senior-year courses before any senior grades exist?
- If your school doesn't rank, what does the college use instead?

### Assessment

Students locate all nine regions on an unfamiliar transcript and explain what at least five of them tell a reader.
