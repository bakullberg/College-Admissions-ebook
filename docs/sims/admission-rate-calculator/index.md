## About This MicroSim

Let students compute admission rates and see why the number of applications, not just the number of admits, drives selectivity.

**Learning objective:** Students will calculate a college's admission rate from its applicant and admit counts and explain how a change in applications changes the rate even when the number of admitted students stays the same.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 1: The College Landscape and Application Rounds](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md).

## How to Use

- Move **Applications received** and **Students admitted**. The formula updates with your numbers, and the icon grid fills one icon per percentage point.
- Read the selectivity band and the "About 1 in N" line under the percentage.
- Press **College A** (40,000 / 6,000) or **College B** (9,000 / 6,300) to load the chapter's worked examples.
- Press **Application surge (+25%)** to raise applications while holding admits constant, and read the caption.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will calculate a college's admission rate from its applicant and admit counts and explain how a change in applications changes the rate even when the number of admitted students stays the same.

**Bloom level:** Apply (L3) — calculate

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 1](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Check the worked examples (3 min):** Load College A and College B and confirm their rates (15% and 70%) by hand.
2. **Predict, then test (4 min):** Before pressing Application surge twice, predict the new rate. Then press it and compare.
3. **Look up a real school (3 min):** Find one college's applicants and admits on its website or the Common Data Set and enter them.

### Discussion Questions

- Why can a college's admission rate fall even when it admits the same number of students?
- Does a 15% admission rate mean you personally have a 15% chance? Why not?
- What band is a college with 25,000 applicants and 5,000 admits in?

### Assessment

Students compute an admission rate correctly from two counts, place it in the right selectivity band, and explain the effect of an application surge in their own words.
