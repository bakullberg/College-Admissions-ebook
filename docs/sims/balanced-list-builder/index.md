## About This MicroSim

Let students sort colleges into reach, target, and safety categories and check whether the resulting list is balanced and financially sound.

**Learning objective:** Students will construct a balanced college list of eight to twelve schools and design it so that at least one affordable college they'd be glad to attend is very likely to admit them.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 3: Finding Your Fit](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md).

## How to Use

- Type a college name, choose its **Category** and **Financial fit**, check **I'd be glad to attend** if true, and press **Add**.
- Click a card to select it, then use **Remove selected**, **◀ Move left**, or **Move right ▶**.
- The **Balance check** updates on every change. The bold line is required: at least one affordable safety you'd be glad to attend.
- Load **Emily's original list** and **Emily's rebalanced list** to compare the chapter's before and after.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will construct a balanced college list of eight to twelve schools and design it so that at least one affordable college they'd be glad to attend is very likely to admit them.

**Bloom level:** Create (L6) — construct, design

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15–20 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 3](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Before and after (4 min):** Load Emily's original list. List every warning, then load the rebalanced list and explain what changed.
2. **Fix a list (5 min):** Start from the original list and use only Move and Remove to clear as many warnings as you can. What can't you fix without adding schools?
3. **Your list (10 min):** Clear the board and build your own list from your research.

### Discussion Questions

- Why must at least one safety be both affordable and a place you'd be glad to attend?
- What makes a list with seven reaches risky, even for a strong student?
- Why is 'Not checked' treated as a warning?

### Assessment

Students build a list that passes every check and name their safest good outcome.
