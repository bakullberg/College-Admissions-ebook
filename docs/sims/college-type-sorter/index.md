## About This MicroSim

Let students practice classifying four-year colleges by funding (public or private) and focus (research university or liberal arts college), and recognize which public research universities are flagships.

**Learning objective:** Students will classify a four-year college as public or private and as a research university or liberal arts college, and explain the clue in its description that decides each label.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 1: The College Landscape and Application Rounds](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md).

## How to Use

- Read the school card at the top, then drag it into the grid cell that matches its funding (column) and focus (row).
- A correct drop snaps in and the feedback names the clue in the profile that decides each label. A wrong drop returns the card with a hint.
- When a public research university lands, answer the **Flagship?** question with the buttons below the grid.
- Type any school into the box and press **Add my own school**. Answer the three questions and press **Place it** to see where it goes and why.
- **Reset** reshuffles the deck.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will classify a four-year college as public or private and as a research university or liberal arts college, and explain the clue in its description that decides each label.

**Bloom level:** Understand (L2) — classify

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 1](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Sort the deck (6 min):** Place all twelve cards. Aim for at least ten on the first try.
2. **Explain one surprise (3 min):** Pick Dartmouth College or St. Mary's College of Maryland and explain in one sentence why its name doesn't decide its category.
3. **Your list (5 min):** Add three schools you are considering. Check each one's website to answer the three questions accurately.

### Discussion Questions

- Why is every flagship a public research university, but not every public research university a flagship?
- Which clue in a profile tells you a school is public?
- Why might a public liberal arts college be rare?

### Assessment

Students sort at least ten of twelve cards correctly on the first try and can name the deciding clue for any card.
