## About This MicroSim

Show students how a college's cost of attendance is built from tuition, room and board, and indirect costs, and how residency and housing choices change the total.

**Learning objective:** Students will break down a cost of attendance into direct and indirect costs and compare how residency and housing choices change the total.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 3: Finding Your Fit](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md).

## How to Use

- Choose **Residency** and **Housing** to fill in Midland State's figures for that scenario.
- Edit any of the five items to enter a different college's published numbers.
- Choose a **Compare against** scenario to draw a second bar, and read the dollar difference below the chart.
- Hover over any segment to see its amount, whether the college bills it, and what it covers. **Reset to Midland State** restores the chapter example.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will break down a cost of attendance into direct and indirect costs and compare how residency and housing choices change the total.

**Bloom level:** Analyze (L4) — break down, compare

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 3](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Read the example (3 min):** Confirm that tuition is about 40% of Midland State's in-state total. What makes up the other 60%?
2. **One change at a time (4 min):** Compare in-state on campus with in-state living with family. Which items change, and by how much?
3. **A real college (6 min):** Find one college's published cost of attendance and enter all five items.

### Discussion Questions

- Why would a student who looks only at tuition underestimate the real cost?
- Which costs on the chart can you control, and which can't you?
- Why do indirect costs still matter even though the college doesn't bill them?

### Assessment

Students break a published cost of attendance into direct and indirect costs and explain how one residency or housing change affects the total.
