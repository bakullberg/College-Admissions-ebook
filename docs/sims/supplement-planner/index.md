## About This MicroSim

Let students enter every supplemental prompt on their list, group the prompts by type, see where material can be adapted, and schedule the writing backward from deadlines.

**Learning objective:** Students will plan their supplemental essays by organizing prompts by type, identifying where core material can be adapted, and scheduling each piece before its deadline.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 10: Supplemental Essays](../../chapters/10-supplemental-essays/index.md).

## How to Use

- Add each prompt with its college, type, word limit, and deadline, then press **Add prompt**.
- **Group view** sorts prompts by type. Community, Major, and Activity prompts share a core essay; Why Us prompts need fresh research.
- **Calendar view** places each piece in a writing week two weeks before its deadline (one week for short answers). Weeks with more than three pieces turn orange.
- Hover over a piece for details and a planning tip, click one to select it for **Remove selected**, and load **Emily's list** for the chapter's 18 pieces. The totals estimate your hours of writing.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will plan their supplemental essays by organizing prompts by type, identifying where core material can be adapted, and scheduling each piece before its deadline.

**Bloom level:** Create (L6) — plan, organize

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15–20 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 10](../../chapters/10-supplemental-essays/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Read Emily's plan (4 min):** Load Emily's list. How many essays does she write fresh, and how many does she adapt?
2. **Find the crunch (4 min):** Switch to Calendar view. Which week is orange, and what would you move earlier?
3. **Your plan (10 min):** Clear the board and enter every supplement on your own list.

### Discussion Questions

- Why can a community essay be adapted but a Why Us essay can't?
- How does seeing every prompt in August change your fall?
- Which pieces would you write first, and why?

### Assessment

Students produce a plan in which no week has more than three pieces and every core essay is scheduled before its adaptations.
