## About This MicroSim

Let students convert between SAT totals and ACT composites using the official concordance and see where a converted score falls in a college's range.

**Learning objective:** Students will use the SAT-ACT concordance to convert a score from one test to the other and place the result within a college's middle 50% range.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 6: The SAT and ACT Question](../../chapters/06-sat-act-question/index.md).

## How to Use

- Choose **SAT** or **ACT** and move the slider to your score. Gold markers show it on both scales.
- Read the conversion line: the matching score and the full concordance range.
- Enter a college's middle 50% range, choose which test it uses, and see where your score falls. The range shows as a blue band.
- Hover over any tick to see its concordance pair. The presets load Emily's, Casey's, and Iowa's merit-threshold examples.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will use the SAT-ACT concordance to convert a score from one test to the other and place the result within a college's middle 50% range.

**Bloom level:** Apply (L3) — convert, use

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 6](../../chapters/06-sat-act-question/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Check the chapter (3 min):** Confirm that SAT 1280 ≈ ACT 27 and that Marcus's 1310 ≈ ACT 28.
2. **Casey's two comparisons (4 min):** Load Casey. Where does a 24 fall in the 1100–1280 range? How far is it from Iowa's 26 merit threshold?
3. **Your score (3 min):** Enter your own score and one college's range from its Common Data Set.

### Discussion Questions

- Why is the ACT scale uneven when it's drawn above the SAT scale?
- Why shouldn't you treat a one-point ACT difference as meaningful?
- When would you need to convert a score at all?

### Assessment

Students convert a score in both directions and correctly place it inside or outside a college's range.
