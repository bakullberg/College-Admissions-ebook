## About This MicroSim

Let students compare retention, four-year graduation, and six-year graduation rates across colleges and interpret the gaps between them.

**Learning objective:** Students will compare retention and graduation rates across colleges and assess which differences signal a reason to investigate further.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 4: Researching and Narrowing Your College List](../../chapters/04-researching-your-list/index.md).

## How to Use

- Each row is one college: its name, retention rate, and four- and six-year graduation rates.
- Type in real numbers from a college's Common Data Set (Section B) or the College Scorecard, or edit the example.
- Check **Show the four-to-six-year gap** to label the difference between the two graduation rates.
- Read the notes panel for automatic flags. Hover over any bar for its definition. **Add college** adds a row (up to four), and **Reset to example** restores the chapter's three fictional colleges.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare retention and graduation rates across colleges and assess which differences signal a reason to investigate further.

**Bloom level:** Evaluate (L5) — compare, assess

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 4](../../chapters/04-researching-your-list/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Read the example (4 min):** Which college has the biggest gap between its four- and six-year rates? What would that cost a student?
2. **Retention as a signal (3 min):** Why does Ridgeview's 81% retention matter even though its six-year rate is 61%?
3. **Real colleges (6 min):** Look up two colleges on your list and enter their real numbers.

### Discussion Questions

- Why can a college with a solid six-year graduation rate still be expensive to finish?
- What questions would you ask on a visit to a college with low retention?
- Why should you compare rates from the same source?

### Assessment

Students identify which flagged differences warrant further investigation and write one question to ask each college about its outcomes.
