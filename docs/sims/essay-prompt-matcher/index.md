## About This MicroSim

Help students test an essay topic against the qualities of a strong personal story and see which Common App prompts it fits, reinforcing that the story comes before the prompt.

**Learning objective:** Students will assess a candidate essay topic against criteria for a strong personal story and select the Common App prompts it fits.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 9: The Personal Essay](../../chapters/09-personal-essay/index.md).

## How to Use

- Type your topic in a few words, then answer the five questions about it.
- The prompt cards light up green for a strong fit and yellow for a possible fit. Prompt 7 always works.
- The **Story strength** meter scores how specific the story is, whether it reveals something new, and whether you know what it means. The tip line suggests a next step, including advice for common topics.
- Try the presets: **Ana — reading with my brother**, **The big game**, and **Translating at the doctor**. Hover over a prompt card for its summary, and read the exact wording on the Common App site.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will assess a candidate essay topic against criteria for a strong personal story and select the Common App prompts it fits.

**Bloom level:** Evaluate (L5) — assess, select

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 9](../../chapters/09-personal-essay/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Ana's story (3 min):** Load Ana's preset. Why does her story fit three prompts?
2. **Rescue the big game (4 min):** Load The big game. Change only the specificity and meaning answers to reach Strong. What would the essay have to be about?
3. **Your topics (6 min):** Test two or three of your own candidate topics. Which one scores Strong?

### Discussion Questions

- Why should you choose the story before the prompt?
- What makes a common topic work anyway?
- Why does a single moment often make a stronger essay than a long summary?

### Assessment

Students evaluate two candidate topics and justify choosing one using specificity, what it reveals, and its meaning.
