## About This MicroSim

Give students a low-pressure way to practice answering common interview questions aloud, with timing and a self-check that encourages specific stories over scripted answers.

**Learning objective:** Students will practice answering common college interview questions aloud and demonstrate answers that use a specific example and stay within a natural length.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 13: Interviews](../../chapters/13-interviews/index.md).

## How to Use

- Read the question, press **Start answering**, and answer out loud. The ring turns green between 30 seconds and 2 minutes, yellow until 3 minutes, then orange.
- Press **Stop**, then check the self-check items that are true. A tip appears for anything unchecked.
- Press **Follow-up** to see a question an interviewer might ask next, and **Next question** for a new card. The deck won't repeat until every card in the category has been shown.
- Filter by category, or check **Interviewer mode** to hide the self-check so a practice partner can read the questions to you.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will practice answering common college interview questions aloud and demonstrate answers that use a specific example and stay within a natural length.

**Bloom level:** Apply (L3) — practice, demonstrate

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15–20 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 13](../../chapters/13-interviews/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Warm up (5 min):** Answer "Tell me about yourself" twice. Aim for under two minutes with one specific story.
2. **Partner practice (10 min):** In Interviewer mode, a partner reads five questions and the follow-ups; switch roles.
3. **Your questions (3 min):** Prepare two genuine questions to ask your interviewer.

### Discussion Questions

- Why do memorized answers sound worse than practiced stories?
- What makes a good question to ask an interviewer?
- How can a follow-up question help you?

### Assessment

Students answer three questions in under two minutes each, using a specific example and saying why it matters to them.
