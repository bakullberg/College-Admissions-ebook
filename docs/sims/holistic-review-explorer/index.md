## About This MicroSim

Show how holistic review produces separate ratings for different parts of an application, and how context changes those ratings, so students see why identical numbers can lead to different outcomes.

**Learning objective:** Students will compare applicant files with similar academic numbers and attribute differences in reader ratings to specific parts of each file.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 2: How Colleges Evaluate Applicants](../../chapters/02-how-colleges-evaluate/index.md).

## How to Use

- Choose **Applicant A**, **Applicant B**, or **Dana**, then click the folder tabs to read each part of the file.
- Change **Activities depth**, **Essay specificity**, or **Recommendation detail** one at a time. The rating that changes flashes gold.
- Uncheck **Reader has the school profile** to see why context matters for a small-school applicant like Dana.
- Hover over any rating box to see what that level means. The model is simplified, and the three ratings are never added together.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare applicant files with similar academic numbers and attribute differences in reader ratings to specific parts of each file.

**Bloom level:** Analyze (L4) — compare, attribute

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 2](../../chapters/02-how-colleges-evaluate/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Same numbers, different files (5 min):** Compare Applicants A and B tab by tab. Which ratings differ, and which parts of the file cause the difference?
2. **One change at a time (5 min):** Starting from Applicant A, change only the essay, then only the recommendations. Which single change helps most?
3. **Context (5 min):** Load Dana and uncheck the school profile. Explain in one sentence why the Academic rating changes.

### Discussion Questions

- Why don't readers add the three ratings into one score?
- Which parts of an application can you still change in senior year?
- What does the school profile tell a reader that the transcript can't?

### Assessment

Students attribute a difference in reader ratings between two files to a specific part of the application and explain the role of context.
