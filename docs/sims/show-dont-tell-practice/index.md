## About This MicroSim

Give students practice recognizing showing versus telling and choosing or writing the more specific version of a sentence.

**Learning objective:** Students will distinguish telling sentences from showing sentences and revise telling sentences into specific, concrete showing sentences.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 9: The Personal Essay](../../chapters/09-personal-essay/index.md).

## How to Use

- Read the telling sentence on the card, then click the revision that shows the quality instead of telling it.
- The feedback explains why the strong version works, what the weak version lacks, and why the overloaded version still tells.
- Press **Next ▶** for a new card. The options are shuffled each round and your score is tallied.
- Press **Write your own** to type a telling sentence and your own showing revision. The checklist looks for concrete words, a number, an action verb, and whether the original adjective is gone.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will distinguish telling sentences from showing sentences and revise telling sentences into specific, concrete showing sentences.

**Bloom level:** Apply (L3) — distinguish, revise

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 9](../../chapters/09-personal-essay/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Play the deck (6 min):** Work through all eight cards and keep your score.
2. **Spot the pattern (3 min):** What do all eight strong revisions have in common?
3. **Your sentence (5 min):** Find one telling sentence in your own essay draft and revise it in Write your own until every check passes.

### Discussion Questions

- Why does piling on adjectives still count as telling?
- What does a number add to a showing sentence?
- Should every sentence in an essay show? Where does telling belong?

### Assessment

Students pick the showing option on at least six of eight cards and revise one of their own telling sentences into a specific showing sentence.
