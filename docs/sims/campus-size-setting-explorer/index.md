## About This MicroSim

Help students see how school size and campus setting combine to shape the typical campus experience, and connect those traits to their own preferences.

**Learning objective:** Students will compare typical campus experiences across combinations of school size and setting and classify colleges they know into the matching cells.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 3: Finding Your Fit](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md).

## How to Use

- Click any cell to read typical classes, typical weekends, and what to watch for.
- Choose **Your preferred size** and **setting** to highlight your target zone in gold. "No preference" highlights a whole row or column.
- Type a college name, pick its size and setting, and press **Place pin** to add it to the map (up to eight). **Clear pins** removes them.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare typical campus experiences across combinations of school size and setting and classify colleges they know into the matching cells.

**Bloom level:** Understand (L2) — compare, classify

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 3](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Compare extremes (3 min):** Read Small rural and Large urban. List two trade-offs between them.
2. **Your zone (3 min):** Set your preferred size and setting from your self-assessment. Read every highlighted cell.
3. **Map your list (4 min):** Pin every college you are considering. Do any fall outside your target zone? Is that a deliberate choice?

### Discussion Questions

- Which 'watch for' item would matter most to you, and why?
- Can a large university feel small? What would you look for?
- Why is a college's setting a different question from its distance from home?

### Assessment

Students correctly place five colleges they know into the grid and describe one typical trade-off of their preferred cell.
