## About This MicroSim

Guide students through a structured self-assessment and turn their answers into a ranked priority profile they can use to evaluate colleges.

**Learning objective:** Students will prioritize their own college criteria across majors, size, setting, culture, and cost, and judge which criteria are dealbreakers and which are trade-offs.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 3: Finding Your Fit](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md).

## How to Use

- Work through the five steps with **Next ▶** and **◀ Back**. Answer each question and set how important it is (1–5).
- Check **This is a dealbreaker** for any factor that would rule a college out on its own.
- The final step ranks your factors. Dealbreakers are locked at the top, and a summary names your top priorities and flexible factors.
- Press **Load Marcus** to see the chapter's worked example, or **Start over** to clear everything.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will prioritize their own college criteria across majors, size, setting, culture, and cost, and judge which criteria are dealbreakers and which are trade-offs.

**Bloom level:** Evaluate (L5) — prioritize, judge

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15–20 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 3](../../chapters/03-finding-your-fit/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Study Marcus (3 min):** Load Marcus and explain why cost is locked at the top even though major is also rated 5.
2. **Your own profile (10 min):** Start over and complete all five steps honestly. Choose at most two dealbreakers.
3. **Test it (5 min):** Name one college you like and check it against your top two priorities and your dealbreakers.

### Discussion Questions

- What is the difference between a dealbreaker and a high priority?
- Why does the profile warn you if you haven't talked with your family about cost?
- Which of your factors could you trade away for a stronger fit on another?

### Assessment

Students produce a ranked profile with no more than two dealbreakers and can justify each dealbreaker in one sentence.
