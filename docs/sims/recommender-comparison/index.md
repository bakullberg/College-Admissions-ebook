## About This MicroSim

Help students compare possible teacher recommenders on the factors that predict a strong letter, and see why "knows me well" outweighs "gave me the highest grade."

**Learning objective:** Students will compare potential recommenders on how well they know the student, recency, subject, and reliability, and choose two teachers whose letters will be specific and complementary.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 11: Letters of Recommendation](../../chapters/11-letters-of-recommendation/index.md).

## How to Use

- Each card shows a teacher's letter-strength bar: how well they know you (50%), recency (20%), core subject (15%), and a reputation for thoughtful, on-time letters (15%). Your grade is shown but not counted.
- Click a card to edit that teacher. **Add teacher** adds a card (up to five) and **Remove selected** deletes one.
- The **Best pair** panel picks two teachers, preferring different subjects when scores are within 10%, and explains why.
- Hover over a bar to see each factor's contribution. Load **Priya's four teachers** or **Two English teachers** to compare the presets.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare potential recommenders on how well they know the student, recency, subject, and reliability, and choose two teachers whose letters will be specific and complementary.

**Bloom level:** Evaluate (L5) — compare, choose

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 11](../../chapters/11-letters-of-recommendation/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Priya's choice (3 min):** Load Priya's four teachers. Why does Mr. Grant beat Ms. Chen even though Priya earned a B in his class?
2. **Variety (3 min):** Load Two English teachers. Why does the tool pick Ms. Cole over Mr. Brooks?
3. **Your teachers (4 min):** Enter the teachers you might ask and compare the result with your instinct.

### Discussion Questions

- Why does how well a teacher knows you matter more than the grade you earned?
- Why are 11th-grade teachers usually the best choice?
- When might a teacher from an elective be a good additional recommender?

### Assessment

Students choose two recommenders for a scenario and justify the choice using how well each knows them and what each letter would add.
