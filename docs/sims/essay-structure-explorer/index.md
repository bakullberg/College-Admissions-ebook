## About This MicroSim

Show how the parts of a personal essay work together under three common structures, and let students plan their own essay with a word budget that totals 650.

**Learning objective:** Students will plan a personal essay by choosing a structure and organizing its parts within a 650-word budget.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 9: The Personal Essay](../../chapters/09-personal-essay/index.md).

## How to Use

- Choose a **Structure**: Narrative arc, Montage, or Idea-driven. The bar splits 650 words among that structure's parts.
- Click any part of the bar to read its job and a sample excerpt. Hover to see its word count and share.
- Drag the sliders to plan your own word budget. The total turns orange over 650 and gray under 500.
- Try the presets: **Ana's outline**, **Too much story**, and **All reflection**.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will plan a personal essay by choosing a structure and organizing its parts within a 650-word budget.

**Bloom level:** Create (L6) — plan, organize

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 9](../../chapters/09-personal-essay/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Ana's plan (3 min):** Click each part of Ana's outline and read the excerpt. Where does the reflection start?
2. **Two bad budgets (4 min):** Load Too much story and All reflection. What would each essay feel like to read?
3. **Your outline (6 min):** Pick a structure for your own essay and set a budget that totals 550–650 words.

### Discussion Questions

- Why does a narrative essay need a turn?
- When would a montage serve a story better than a narrative arc?
- Why is an essay under 500 words usually missing reflection?

### Assessment

Students produce an outline with a named structure, a job for each part, and a word budget between 550 and 650.
