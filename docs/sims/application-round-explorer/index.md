## About This MicroSim

Help students choose an application round for one college by weighing certainty, readiness, and the need to compare financial aid, and show the consequences of each choice.

**Learning objective:** Students will judge which application round (ED, EA, REA, RD, or rolling) best fits a given student and college, and justify the choice using whether the round is binding and whether it allows comparing aid offers.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 1: The College Landscape and Application Rounds](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md).

## How to Use

- Check the rounds the college offers, then answer the four questions about the student.
- Read the recommendation, the explanation, and what an admission would commit you to.
- The question that decided the recommendation is highlighted in gold.
- The timeline marks the round's deadline, decision date, and commitment point between September and May 1.
- Load **Sam**, **Iowa applicant**, or **Certain first choice, cost checked** to see the chapter's examples. Red warnings flag rules you would break.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will judge which application round (ED, EA, REA, RD, or rolling) best fits a given student and college, and justify the choice using whether the round is binding and whether it allows comparing aid offers.

**Bloom level:** Evaluate (L5) — judge, recommend

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 1](../../chapters/01-college-landscape-and-rounds/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Check the chapter's examples (4 min):** Load all three scenarios and say in your own words why each round fits.
2. **Change one answer (5 min):** Start from Sam and change only the aid question to No and first choice to Yes. Explain what flips and why.
3. **Your own college (6 min):** Look up one college on your list, check its real rounds, and run your own answers.

### Discussion Questions

- Why does needing to compare financial aid rule out Early Decision?
- When is Regular Decision a stronger choice than any early round?
- What rule do you break by applying Early Decision to two colleges?

### Assessment

Students justify a recommended round for a new scenario using two criteria: whether the round is binding and whether it lets the family compare aid offers.
