## About This MicroSim

Let students compare up to four colleges' award letters on net price, out-of-pocket cost, four-year cost, and debt, so the real cost of each offer is visible side by side.

**Learning objective:** Students will compare multiple financial aid offers on first-year net price, out-of-pocket cost, projected four-year cost, and total debt, and judge which offers are affordable for their family.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 16: Comparing Award Letters](../../chapters/16-comparing-award-letters/index.md).

## How to Use

- Enter each college's cost, grants and scholarships, work-study, student federal loans, Parent PLUS, and other loans. Check **Includes merit that won't rise** for fixed merit awards.
- Set your family's yearly contribution and the yearly cost increase. Check **Grants rise with cost?** if a college's need-based grants grow with its costs (ask each aid office).
- The first chart compares year 1; the second stacks four years of net price against your four-year budget line.
- The table shows four-year totals and whether each offer fits your budget or needs Parent PLUS. **Reset to Marcus** restores the chapter's three colleges. On narrow screens, pick which college to edit and which chart to see.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will compare multiple financial aid offers on first-year net price, out-of-pocket cost, projected four-year cost, and total debt, and judge which offers are affordable for their family.

**Bloom level:** Evaluate (L5) — compare, judge

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15–20 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 16](../../chapters/16-comparing-award-letters/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Marcus's three (5 min):** With the defaults, which college costs least over four years? Which needs Parent PLUS, and in which years?
2. **Rising grants (4 min):** Check Grants rise with cost. How does Brookfield's status change? Why does it matter that Brookfield meets full need?
3. **Your offers (8 min):** Enter your own award letters and decide which are affordable.

### Discussion Questions

- Why can a small first-year gap become a large four-year gap?
- Why do merit scholarships usually lose value over four years?
- What should you ask each aid office before you decide?

### Assessment

Students compare three offers on four-year net price and borrowing and justify which are affordable for a given family contribution.
