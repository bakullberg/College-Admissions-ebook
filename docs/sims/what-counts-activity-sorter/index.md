## About This MicroSim

Challenge the assumption that only school-sponsored activities count, by having students classify real-life examples and see which activity type each would be listed under.

**Learning objective:** Students will classify everyday commitments as listable activities or not and explain which activity type each belongs to and why.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 8: Activities and Resume](../../chapters/08-activities-and-resume/index.md).

## How to Use

- Read the scenario card and press **List it** or **Probably not**.
- Read the feedback: the recommended answer, the Common App activity type, and the reason.
- Gray-area cards show **It depends**, with what would make them count. They aren't scored.
- Press **Next ▶** for the next card and **Restart** to shuffle the deck. The tally collects the activity types you've discovered.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will classify everyday commitments as listable activities or not and explain which activity type each belongs to and why.

**Bloom level:** Understand (L2) — classify, explain

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 8](../../chapters/08-activities-and-resume/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Play the deck (6 min):** Work through all fourteen cards.
2. **Gray areas (2 min):** For the video games card, describe a version that would clearly count.
3. **Your hidden activities (2 min):** List one commitment of your own that you hadn't thought of as an activity.

### Discussion Questions

- Why do family responsibilities count as activities?
- What makes a paid summer program weaker than a part-time job on an application?
- When does a hobby become an activity?

### Assessment

Students correctly classify at least nine of the eleven clear-cut cards and explain one gray-area card's condition.
