## About This MicroSim

Help students practice separating worries they can act on from worries they can't during the waiting period, and turn each controllable worry into a small next step.

**Learning objective:** Students will classify common waiting-period worries as within or outside their control and plan one concrete action for each controllable worry.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 17: Waiting and Managing Anxiety](../../chapters/17-waiting-and-anxiety/index.md).

## How to Use

- Read the worry, then choose **I can do something about this** or **Out of my hands**.
- Read the feedback. For worries you can act on, edit the suggested next step and press **Save step**.
- **My plan** collects every step you save and counts the worries you let go. It's kept in this browser when storage is available.
- Press **Next ▶** for the next worry, or **Start over** to clear your plan and reshuffle.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will classify common waiting-period worries as within or outside their control and plan one concrete action for each controllable worry.

**Bloom level:** Apply (L3) — classify, plan

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 17](../../chapters/17-waiting-and-anxiety/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Sort the deck (6 min):** Work through all fourteen worries and save at least three steps.
2. **Schedule it (2 min):** Put each saved step into your calendar with a day and time.
3. **Let go (2 min):** Pick one worry from the Out of my hands pile and write what you'll do instead when it comes up.

### Discussion Questions

- Why does checking the portal ten times a day make waiting harder?
- Which of your worries has a next step you could take this week?
- Who can you talk to when the waiting feels heavy?

### Assessment

Students sort the deck and produce a plan with at least three concrete, scheduled next steps.
