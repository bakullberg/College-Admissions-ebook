## About This MicroSim

Help students compare two to four finalist colleges with a weighted decision matrix that combines cost, fit, and outcomes, and see which factors drive the result.

**Learning objective:** Students will weigh their finalist colleges on cost, academic fit, social fit, outcomes, and personal sense using weights that reflect their own priorities, and justify a final choice by identifying the factors that most influence it.

This MicroSim supports [Chapter 19: Making Your Final Choice](../../chapters/19-making-your-final-choice/index.md).

## How to Use

- Type your finalists' names, set a weight (0–5) for each factor, and score each college 1–5.
- Enter four-year net prices to score cost automatically (lowest price = 5), or check **Override cost scores** to score cost yourself.
- Each college's bar is split by factor; the leader is outlined in gold. **What's driving this?** names the two factors with the biggest weighted difference between the top two.
- Use **Add factor** for your own criteria and **Add college** or **Remove college** for up to four finalists. Load the Priya or Marcus preset to see the chapter's examples. Hover over a bar segment for weight × score.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

Students will weigh their finalist colleges on cost, academic fit, social fit, outcomes, and personal sense using weights that reflect their own priorities, and justify a final choice by identifying the factors that most influence it.

**Bloom level:** Evaluate (L5) — weigh, justify

### Audience

High school juniors and seniors, and the parents, counselors, and mentors helping them.

### Duration

15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Read the section of [Chapter 19](../../chapters/19-making-your-final-choice/index.md) that introduces this MicroSim.

### Activities

1. **Priya's matrix (4 min):** Load Priya's preset and confirm 95 vs. 86. Which factors favor the flagship?
2. **Change the weights (4 min):** Raise Cost to 5 and lower Gut sense to 2. Does the leader change? What does that tell you?
3. **Your finalists (7 min):** Enter your own finalists and weights, then compare the total with your gut.

### Discussion Questions

- Why isn't the highest total automatically the right choice?
- What does it mean when your gut disagrees with the total?
- Which factor would you never trade away?

### Assessment

Students build a matrix for their finalists and justify their choice by naming the factors that drive the result.
